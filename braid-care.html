How long do braids last?

How long a style looks fresh varies with the braid pattern, your hair and your everyday routine. Agree a removal plan when choosing your style.

The British Association of Dermatologists recommends keeping braids in for no longer than six weeks. Some styles or scalps need an earlier take-down. Remove them sooner if they are pulling, painful or becoming difficult to manage.

Ask us about the upkeep involved before deciding on your next look, especially if it is your first braided style.

Can I wash my hair while it is braided?

Yes. Your scalp and natural hair still need cleansing. Wash when needed rather than leaving product build-up in place to preserve the style. The right frequency depends on your scalp and the products you use.

Use a gentle shampoo and your fingertips, with light handling rather than vigorous rubbing or pulling. Rinse thoroughly and condition the accessible lengths of your natural hair. Gently blot with a towel and allow time for the roots and braids to dry fully. If using a dryer, choose a low heat and power setting.

See the NHS good hair care advice for gentle washing, conditioning and drying guidance.

How should I sleep with braids?

Keep your braids gathered loosely and use a comfortable satin or silk bonnet, scarf or pillowcase. Avoid a tight ponytail or bun underneath the covering, and make sure the edge of a bonnet is not pulling on your hairline.

A smooth covering can reduce friction, but comfort matters too. The American Academy of Dermatology recommends loose styling beneath silk or satin coverings.

How do I care for my scalp and natural hair?

Keep your routine simple: regular cleansing and a moisturiser suited to your hair. Use products according to their instructions, and avoid repeatedly layering products over build-up. Oils and styling gels do not replace washing.

Follow any existing care advice you have been given for a scalp condition. If a product causes irritation, stop using it. For everyday wash-day products and accessories, browse our haircare range or ask us about the options when enquiring.

How should freshly braided hair feel?

Fresh braids may feel snug at first, especially during the first few days. They should still feel comfortable, without pain or excessive pulling on your scalp. Keep your braids loose rather than tying them into a tight ponytail or bun.

Tell us straight away if you feel pain, stinging or strong pulling, or notice scalp crusting or skin lifting around the braids, so we can check the style and adjust the tension. A painful style needs loosening or removal promptly; do not wait for pain to settle over two or three days.

Repeated excessive tension can damage hair and cause hair loss. For ongoing irritation, broken skin or signs of hair loss, ask a GP or dermatologist for advice. Read the AAD guidance on comfortable styling and scalp health.

How should I take my braids out?

Set aside enough time to undo the style carefully. Work in sections and use your fingers to ease out tangles before gentle detangling with a wide-tooth comb. Avoid pulling through knots or cutting anywhere near your natural hair.

Cleanse and condition after removal, and check how your hairline and scalp feel before choosing another style. Give your hair a break from tension between braided looks. If you are unsure about the take-down, ask us for guidance before starting.
